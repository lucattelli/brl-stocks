## Facts learned

- F1 — No free real-time source for B3 was found; a 15-minute delay is the realistic free ceiling. US real time is free with a key (Finnhub, Alpaca). (`market-data-sources-2026-11.md` §2.2, §4)
- F2 — Yahoo's unofficial chart endpoint is the only free, keyless API found that covers both B3 and US. Tested 2026-11-23 with a stock, a fractional ticker, a FII, an ETF, a BDR, ^BVSP and AAPL. Measured B3 lag: 904 to 1053 s; USD/BRL about 10 s. (§2.1)
- F3 — Yahoo limits: one request per ticker (the multi-symbol endpoint needs cookie and crumb); FIIs, ETFs and BDRs are all labelled `EQUITY`; it is unofficial and has had waves of 429 blocks. (§2.1, §2.3)
- F4 — brapi.dev free tier: 15,000 requests/month, one ticker per request, quotes updated every 30 minutes, 3 months of history (about 20 tickers every 30 minutes). Paid from R$ 99.99/month billed yearly for 15-minute updates. Terms allow display in your own product but forbid proxies or wrappers. (§2.3)
- F5 — B3's own site endpoint (`cotacao.b3.com.br/mds/api/v1/instrumentQuotation/<ticker>`) works without a key, 15 minutes delayed, but is undocumented. The official COTAHIST daily file is public and end of day. (§2.3)
- F6 — Poor fits: Stooq (CAPTCHA and JavaScript check), Alpha Vantage (25 requests/day), Twelve Data (B3 end of day only, from US$ 29), Massive, formerly Polygon (US only, real time at US$ 199). (§2.2)
- F7 — [unverified] Finnhub and Financial Modeling Prep pricing could not be read; HG Brasil (400 requests/day free) does not publish its delay. (§3)
- F8 — No existing tool covers B3 and US with all three run modes (D2, D6). No B3-focused terminal monitor was found. (`existing-stock-tuis-2026-11.md` §2.4, §4)
- F9 — `ticker` (Go, Bubble Tea, GPL-3.0, 6,270 stars, v5.3.0 of 2026-06-21) is the closest: `.SA` symbols confirmed by its maintainer in 2021, groups, BRL conversion. Gaps: its print mode outputs holdings only, not the watchlist (requested since 2021–22); no index or exchange-rate header, no alerts, no in-app editing, no provider layer (requested since 2021). B3 support is from docs and issues, not tested. (§2.3, §2.4)

(§2.1, §2.3)
- F11 — 10 of 14 compared tools depend on Yahoo, and it is their main source of bug reports: about four breakage waves from 2023 to 2025 (ticker took about 33 days to fix one; tickrs had no data for about five months). The breaks hit the batch quote endpoint (cookie and crumb); the chart endpoint mostly survived. (§2.5, §3)

- F13 — One request to Yahoo's chart endpoint (`interval=5m&range=1d`) returns everything a row needs: last price, previous close, day low and high, name, currency, quote time, and the intraday series for the sparkline. No extra request per ticker for D13. (own test, 2026-11-23 14:41 UTC, PETR4.SA and AAPL)

- F15 — Measured during open markets: B3 quote lag 902 s (about 15 minutes); US quote lag 1 s, so US quotes from this source are effectively real time. (own test, 2026-11-23 14:41 UTC)
- F16 — `~/the-2026-dev-env` is the user's dev-environment repo. Its convention: one folder per tool at the repo root, linked as a whole directory by `bin/devenv-setup` with `link "$repo/<tool>" "$HOME/.config/<tool>"` (nvim, tmux, mise, alacritty are linked this way). (local inspection, `bin/devenv-setup` lines 33–46 and 368–371, 2026-11-23)
- F17 — The `the-2026-dev-env` repo is public on GitHub, so a watchlist kept there is public. (`gh repo view`, 2026-11-23)
- F18 — Bubble Tea version 1 was last released as v1.3.10 on 2025-09-17; version 2 is at v2.1.2, released 2026-11-18, under the module path `charm.land/bubbletea/v2`. (Go module proxy, queried 2026-11-23)
- F19 — Design exploration: three independent designs converged on a heartbeat over the wall clock, one shared refresh path, a JSON cache written by rename with lock-free readers, per-entry back-off stored in the cache, and typed provider errors. No design had a fatal finding; every serious finding was conceded and fixed. (`design/synthesis.md` §1, §2)
- F20 — The same exploration found behaviour the spec does not define: what `r` does while the source is blocking, what an unknown ticker shows, when a row counts as stale, and whether a corrupt cache is reported. (`design/synthesis.md` §4)
- F21 — `syscall.Flock` cannot be cancelled and is Unix-only; a design that locks the cache supports Linux and macOS, not Windows. (`design/critique-extensible-claude.md`, fact check)
- F22 — Sources write B3 tickers differently: Yahoo `PETR4.SA`, brapi `PETR4`, HG Brasil `B3:PETR4`; Alpha Vantage `.SAO` is [unverified]. (`market-data-sources-2026-11.md` §2.4)
- F23 — Attack on the converged design by another model family (Codex, two rounds): round 1 found 2 fatal and 7 serious faults, round 2 found 2 fatal and 6 serious in the revision. With a healthy source, an intact cache and one watchlist the design holds QR4 (230 to 253 requests in ten minutes against 345) and QR5 (9 against 10). The faults are in the refresh protocol's edges: what a stamp means, where the block record lives, a lock holder that stalls, and how close, open and holidays are guessed. (`design/synthesis.md` §8, §9)
- F24 — A `flock` is released on explicit unlock, when the last descriptor of the open file closes, or when the process dies; a stopped process keeps it. `flock` exists on Linux, macOS and the BSDs, not on Windows. Corrects the platform wording of F21. (`design/critique-converged-codex.md` and `-2.md`, fact checks, citing flock(2))
- F25 — In Go, a struct used as a JSON map key needs both `MarshalText` and `UnmarshalText`; with only the first, the cache can be written and not read back. (`design/critique-converged-codex.md`, citing the `encoding/json` documentation)
- F26 — [unverified here; reported by the Codex critic with B3's trading calendar as source] B3 opened at 13:00 on Ash Wednesday 2026, so a session can start hours later than the usual time. (`design/critique-converged-codex-2.md`, finding 5)
- F27 — For a ticker it does not know, the source answers HTTP 404 with a JSON body whose error code is "Not Found" ("No data found, symbol may be delisted"). So a 404 with that body is "not found", not a refusal. (own test, `XXXX9.SA`, 2026-11-23 21:47 UTC)
- F28 — After the US close, the source's regular price does not follow after-hours trading: 47 minutes into the post-market session, AAPL's regular price equalled the last regular bar and its quote time was 21:00:01 UTC, one second after the regular period's end. One sample. (own test, 2026-11-23 21:47 UTC)
- F29 — For USD/BRL (`BRL=X`) the source reports a regular period of 00:00 to 23:59 UTC for the day, in the Europe/London zone, with no pre or post period; bars ran from 00:20 UTC. Weekend behaviour is [unverified]. (own test, 2026-11-23 21:47 UTC)
- F30 — For B3 the source reports a regular period of 13:00 to 20:00 UTC (10:00 to 17:00 in São Paulo) and a post period to 21:00 UTC, and its intraday bars stop at 20:00 UTC. But at 21:47 UTC the quote time of PETR4 was 21:05:45 UTC and that of the Ibovespa 21:17 UTC: more than an hour after the reported end. So the quote time passes the session end, and the final B3 quote can arrive more than 80 minutes after it, counting the 15-minute delay. Whether the price itself still moves after 20:00 UTC, and whether the reported period is simply an hour short in this season, are [unverified]. (own test, 2026-11-23 21:47 UTC)
- F31 — Third attack round, on the approved design's refresh protocol (Codex): 3 fatal and 6 serious faults. Rows claimed before a refusal were still requested after it; an expired claim's holder could overwrite a newer quote; a process stopped inside the lock blocked the others without limit. With one watchlist and ordinary timing the protocol holds QR4 (230 to 253 requests against 345) and QR5 (9 against 10). With two watchlists on one cache, two `print` loops can send about 3,300 requests in ten minutes. (`design/synthesis.md` §10, `design/critique-design-codex-3.md`)
- F32 — Verification of the built `print` binary against a local stand-in (a fresh agent, spec only; 43 checks): every numbered criterion it could check passed. It found one fault, fixed (rows whose requests were in flight when the first refusal arrived were not marked stale, because the tool gave those requests up), and one gap, now D73. Observed: ten simultaneous calls on an empty cache send one request per instrument; 15 forced calls in 30 seconds during a block send none; 5 minutes 8 seconds after a refusal the next call sends one probe and then the rest; a fresh cache is printed in 4 ms with no request. Not verified from outside: the full-screen view, the 8-hour criterion, QR4 and QR5 at their full ten minutes, the wait doubling after a second refusal. (`verification/README.md`, 2026-11-24)
